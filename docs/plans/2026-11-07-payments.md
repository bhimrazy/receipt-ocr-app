# Payments: getting paid in Nepal from subscribers anywhere (2026-11-07)

_Research · status: proposed, nothing built, nothing signed up for_

The owner is an individual developer in Nepal with no foreign company. The question: which provider lets anyone in
any country subscribe to Pro, and still pays out into a Nepali bank account legally. Facts below were checked on
2026-11-07 against provider docs and Nepali sources; anything not confirmed is marked **unverified**. Tax and foreign
exchange points are a starting brief, not advice: **verify with a Nepali chartered accountant before the first
payout.**

## Recommendation

**Apply to Paddle first, as merchant of record (MoR), for international subscriptions. Keep Dodo Payments as the
fallback, and look at Creem only if both turn us down.** Paddle supports Nepal as a seller country (it isn't on the
short unsupported list), pays out by SWIFT wire or Payoneer, handles VAT and sales tax worldwide, and has the most
mature subscription engine and sandbox. Lemon Squeezy and Polar are out: Nepal is on neither payout list, and Lemon
Squeezy is moving into Stripe Managed Payments, which needs a Stripe-supported country. Skip Stripe Atlas for now. A
Nepali resident owning a US LLC runs into Nepal's outward-investment ban, and the US filings cost more than early
revenue brings in. For Nepali customers paying in NPR, **add Khalti (or eSewa) one-time checkout later, sold as
prepaid Pro months.** No Nepali wallet offers card-style recurring billing yet. Fonepay announced "Autopay" in
October 2026, so check it again then. Hide the provider behind one billing service in `packages/core` so that
switching MoR later costs one adapter, not a rewrite.

Two things to settle early. Paddle's pricing page says **transactions under $10 "may require custom pricing"**, so
pick the Pro price (CEO review: $9/mo or $90/yr) with that in mind and ask Paddle about it during onboarding. And the
Nepali bank must record each payout as **IT service export income**, not as remittance, to get the 5% tax treatment.

The fixed fee is what hurts at $5: it costs 8–10 points on its own. **An annual plan ($50–90/yr) brings the fee
down to ~5–6% and gets past Paddle's sub-$10 rule.** The Nepali bank's USD→NPR spread comes on top and isn't
published (**unverified**, likely under 1%).

## Stripe directly, via Stripe Atlas: not now

- **Cost:** Atlas is $500 once (Delaware incorporation, EIN, first year of registered agent), then $100/yr for the
  agent. On top: the Delaware annual tax and report, US federal filings for a foreign-owned company (Form 5472 with a
  pro forma 1120 for a single-member LLC; the penalty for missing it is large), a US bank account, and an accountant.
  Expect roughly $1,000+/yr before the first sale (**estimate**, not quoted).
- **Tax burden:** Stripe direct isn't an MoR, so we'd owe EU VAT, UK VAT and US state sales tax ourselves (Stripe Tax
  calculates it for a fee, but we still register and file). Stripe Managed Payments is Stripe's MoR, but it needs a
  business in one of ~37 supported countries (Unity docs, Sept 2026). Nepal isn't one, so it still means Atlas.
- **Nepal law:** the Act Restricting Investment Abroad (1964) still bars residents from investing abroad or holding
  foreign bank accounts without approval. NRB's 2025 relaxations (June 2025: IT companies with 3 years of exports,
  up to 50% of earnings / $1M; Dec 30 2025: any **Nepali company** up to $20,000 without prior approval) cover
  **companies, not individuals**. An individual-owned US LLC is a legal risk until a lawyer says otherwise.
- **When to revisit:** MoR fees past ~$3–5k MRR, a Nepali company with export history (then the outward-investment
  route opens), or a need for Stripe-only features.

## Nepali customers in NPR (later)

- **Khalti** (ePayment web checkout, KPG-2) and **eSewa** (ePay v2) are redirect-and-verify, one-time payments with
  test environments. Neither documents saved-wallet recurring billing. Khalti's merchant terms forbid storing wallet
  credentials.
- **Registration:** both need a registered business. For a sole proprietorship that means a Banijya/Udhyog
  registration certificate, PAN/VAT certificate, a tax clearance certificate and the proprietor's ID (Khalti merchant
  terms). Khalti charges a one-time API fee, a per-transaction % and (per its terms) an annual fee from year 2. Get
  the numbers from their sales team.
- **Shape:** sell **"Pro for 1 / 3 / 12 months"** as one-time purchases that extend `plan_expires_at`, and email a
  renewal reminder. Plan state is then the same as for an MoR subscription (see below).
- **Watch:** Fonepay "Autopay" for recurring payments (announced 2026-10-13, no merchant docs found), and NCHL's
  NEPALPAY Request (connectIPS request-to-pay; needs authorization for each debit).

## Nepal side: receiving the money (verify with an accountant)

- **Channel:** foreign income has to come in through a licensed bank. An MoR payout by SWIFT wire into a Nepali bank
  account is the clean path. Payoneer is legal and widely used; it withdraws to a Nepali bank in NPR, with fees "up
  to 3%" including conversion (Payoneer pricing). **PayPal still can't receive in Nepal** (2026 sources; NRB has
  talked to PayPal, but there is no agreement). Wise accounts aren't open to Nepali residents.
- **Classification:** ask the bank to tag each inbound wire as **IT/software service export** and keep each payout
  statement from the MoR as the invoice. The Kathmandu Post (2026-02-25) notes inflows often get logged as remittance
  instead.
- **Tax:** resident individuals exporting IT services pay **5% tax, deducted by the bank on receipt and final** (Finance
  Act 2082 per several 2025–26 summaries; Khalti's Stripe FAQ also applies 5% TDS against your PAN). Sources disagree
  on a cap (one says final up to NPR 40 lakh) and mention a separate 75% exemption. **Confirm the current Finance Act
  2083 text with an accountant.**
- **PAN:** get an individual PAN before the first payout, so the 5% is recorded against you. A business registration
  isn't needed for the MoR route (**verify**), only for local gateways.
- **VAT:** exported services are zero-rated. VAT registration is mandatory past a turnover threshold (NPR 30 lakh in
  2025 sources; **verify**). The MoR collects foreign VAT; we don't.
- **Forex card limit:** service exporters can spend their foreign earnings + $500 a year on cards, up to $10,000
  (NRB amendment reported 2026-10-06). That's useful for paying Gemini, Vercel and Supabase.

## What the owner does to start (no code needed)

1. **PAN** (individual) at IRD, if not held already; tell your bank you'll receive IT export income in USD. Ask
   whether they need a USD account and what spread they charge.
2. **Make the site ready for MoR review:** live HTTPS site, a published price, Terms, Privacy and a **Refund policy**
   linked from the nav, and your legal name (or brand) in the terms. `/terms` and `/privacy` exist; a refund policy and
   the public price don't yet.
3. **Paddle:** sign up (sandbox works at once), submit the domain review and identity check (exact documents for a
   Nepali individual **unverified**; have a passport or citizenship ID and proof of address ready), add bank SWIFT details. Ask about the sub-$10 fee. Expect a few days for review.
4. If Paddle declines or reprices: **Dodo Payments** (government ID; 24–72 h review), then Creem.
5. **Accountant:** 5% final-tax eligibility, the D-01/D-04 return, VAT threshold, record-keeping.
6. **Later, for NPR:** register a sole proprietorship (Banijya), get PAN/VAT, then apply to Khalti or eSewa.

## Integration fit

Plan state is per **user** today (`user.plan`, `packages/db/src/auth-schema.ts`; limits from `policyFor` in
`packages/core/src/plans.ts`), and the workspace owner's plan drives workspace limits. Paddle, Dodo and Creem all have
hosted checkout, signed webhooks, a customer portal and a sandbox or test mode, so the integration looks the same for
each:

```mermaid
sequenceDiagram
  participant U as User (owner)
  participant W as Next.js app
  participant M as MoR (Paddle)
  participant Q as Job queue (packages/core/jobs)
  participant DB as Postgres
  participant B as Nepali bank
  U->>W: Upgrade to Pro
  W->>M: Create checkout (customData: userId)
  M-->>U: Hosted/overlay checkout, tax added
  U->>M: Pays
  M->>W: Webhook subscription.created / transaction.completed (signed)
  W->>W: Verify signature, store raw event (unique event id)
  W->>Q: enqueue billing.apply_event
  W-->>M: 200
  Q->>DB: Upsert subscription (status, plan, period end)
  Q->>DB: user.plan = pro while active / past_due in grace
  Note over M,DB: renewals, past_due, canceled → same path; cancel = Free at period end
  M->>B: Monthly payout by SWIFT (bank deducts 5% TDS)
```

- **Tables:** `billing_subscription` (provider, provider ids, `user_id`, status, plan, `current_period_end`,
  `cancel_at_period_end`) and `billing_event` (provider event id unique, for idempotency and replay).
  `user.plan` stays the one field that limits read. Admin "set plan" stays as a comp override, recorded separately so
  a webhook doesn't wipe it.
- **Route:** `apps/web/app/api/billing/webhook/route.ts` as a thin adapter: verify the signature, insert the event,
  enqueue a job kind, return 200. Apply the event in `packages/core` (AGENTS.md: no unrecorded `after()`).
- **Provider boundary:** a `Billing` Effect service with a small adapter interface (`createCheckout`, `portalUrl`,
  `parseWebhook`) so Paddle → Dodo → Stripe stays a swap. Prepaid NPR purchases from Khalti go through the same
  `apply_event` path with a fixed period end.
- **Docs:** the implementing PR adds the billing flow and tables to `docs/architecture/`.

## Risks

- **Approval or a reprice at $5–9** (Paddle's sub-$10 rule; MoRs reject thin or "AI wrapper" sites). Mitigate with a
  complete site, an annual plan and a fallback MoR.
- **Payout friction in Nepal:** SWIFT fees and bank spreads at low volume; Paddle's $100 minimum delays the first
  payout. The bank may log a payout as remittance and miss the 5% treatment.
- **Provider churn:** Lemon Squeezy is being absorbed into Stripe; Creem and Dodo are 2024–25 startups. The adapter
  boundary and webhook event log keep a migration manageable (subscriptions usually need customers to re-enter cards,
  so plan migrations around renewals).
- **Regulatory change:** NRB rules moved several times in 2025–26 (outward investment, card limits, Khalti × Stripe).
  Some of it may get easier (international gateway policy), and some may tighten.
- **Tax figures** come from secondary Nepali sources that disagree on caps and exemptions. Accountant first.
