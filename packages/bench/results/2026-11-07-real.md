31 real receipts (5 CORD, 4 Wikimedia Commons, 6 of the owner's Nepal receipts, 11 web receipts, 5 AI-generated) plus the 6 synthetic fixtures, each sent as the original file (`none`) and as the app uploads it (`app`). Scores are field accuracy from `bench/score.ts`. Only scores and sizes are recorded here; the private sets' images and extracted text stay off the repo.

Spend for the whole run, including failed Gemini calls (free): **$2.55**.

## Findings

- **Haiku 5.5 and Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite are as accurate as Sonnet on these receipts** at a fraction of the cost: about $0.0006 (Haiku) and $0.0014 (Flash-Lite) per receipt against $0.011 (Sonnet) and $0.022 (Opus). Haiku is also the fastest (about 2 s).
- **Dates are the weak field for every model** (65–90%). On the owner's Nepal receipts Haiku and Sonnet got 1 of 6: Bikram Sambat-only receipts came back `null`, and Nepali POS dates printed month-first (`10/07/2023`) were read day-first. Opus does better only because it sometimes converts BS itself. Fix in code, not with a bigger model: see the dual-calendar dates plan.
- **The app's shrinking costs no meaningful accuracy** and makes calls 0.3–0.5 s faster.
- **Gemini 3.8 Flash has no result**: the free-tier key allows 20 requests a day and Google also returned 503 (overloaded). Re-run on a paid key.
- **Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite failed every call before #84** (Google rejects `maxItems` in its response schema; the 400 was misread as a bad photo). These numbers are from the re-run after the fix.
- Item extraction (87–91%) is the next accuracy target: handwritten bill books and long receipts.

## Suggested default chain

Claude Haiku 5.5, then Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite (two providers, so one outage doesn't stop reads), with Sonnet as an optional last step for receipts both fail. Revisit after the dual-calendar date change and a paid-key Gemini 3.8 Flash run.
